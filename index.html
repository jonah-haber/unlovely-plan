EP Cycle Plan

Nov 2026 – May 2027 · Last updated October 29, 2026

A 4–5 song EP (3 from the backlog, 1–2 new) recorded Jan–Feb and released as a singles waterfall from March, ending in an EP release show around May.

How to use this

Each item sits in the month it should start. Its due date is the rehearsal (Wednesday or Sunday) it has to be done by, so every rehearsal doubles as a check-in. Items marked at rehearsal get done together at that rehearsal. Releases stay on Fridays, when streaming services publish new music, and shows are on Saturdays. Every item gets one owner, and items marked gate block something later, so protect those dates first.

Monthly kickoff. The first rehearsal of each month assigns owners for that month's items only, nothing further out. Then the band spends two minutes on next month's list with one question: does anything here need someone booked or started now? Only those items get an owner early; everything else waits for its own month. A loose point person per area (visuals, press, admin, fans and shows) stays steady across months, so outside helpers always have one contact.

For each role: write a one-paragraph brief (what, when, budget, credit), confirm availability for the whole window, agree on price or trade in writing, and set a first deadline. Paying friends fairly, or at least on time and with credit, is what keeps them available for the next cycle.

Producer agreement checklist. No lawyer needed, but these are the points one would make sure are written down, even between friends. A one-page email that everyone replies "agreed" to is enough.

  • Scope: tracking, editing, mixing and mastering for 4–5 songs, plus alt mixes (instrumental, clean) and stems.
  • Payment: flat fee, per song, deferred, or producer "points" (a share of master royalties). Say which, how much and when it's paid.
  • Ownership: the band owns the masters and the session files, and gets copies of the raw sessions.
  • Songwriting: the producer isn't a co-writer unless he writes parts; if he does, put him on the split sheets.
  • Credit: exactly how he's credited on releases and posts.
  • Revisions and schedule: number of mix revision rounds, and the delivery dates in this plan.
  • Future use: whether he approves or shares in remixes, sync licensing or re-releases.
  • If plans change: what happens to unfinished songs and payment if the project stops partway.

What to outsource, and where it stalls

Keep the decisions in the band and hand off the craft work.

Keep in-house: song choices, split sheets, mix and master sign-off, rollout calendar, distributor and metadata, mailing list, posting.

Outsource first: cover art and single covers, press photos, music video, short-form clip editing, EP press kit writing. These take the most hours and benefit most from skilled hands.

Most likely bottlenecks

  • Producer agreement and schedule. Without dates and scope on paper, everything slides. Settle it in November.
  • Mix revisions. Agree on a number of rounds and a deadline for notes, or mixing can drag on for months.
  • Cover art. It's needed before the first single can be uploaded and pitched. Commission it in December so the single 1 cover is ready by Wed Jan 27.
  • New-lineup photos. Every visual depends on them. Shoot in December, before the holidays and tracking.
  • Distributor lead time. Upload each single several weeks before release so there's time to pitch it.
  • One person doing all of it. If every task lands on the same person, nothing moves. Owners get assigned at every monthly kickoff, so no one carries everything.
